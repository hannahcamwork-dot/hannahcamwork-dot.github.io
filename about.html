About

Studying accounting. Practising it where I can.

I’m working toward a Bachelor of Commerce (Honours) in Accounting, with a minor in Finance, at Ontario Tech University. Alongside the degree, I’ve prepared personal tax returns as a volunteer with the CRA’s tax program, kept the books for small-business clients during a co-op term, and looked after the finances of a student organization.

01Context

A little more context

Most of the rules arrived with an example already attached.

I expected my first taxation course to be dense and technical, and it is. What I didn’t expect was how much of it I had already seen. Four filing seasons of matching slips to returns, asking clients for what was missing, and comparing Notices of Assessment against returns I had prepared meant most of the rules arrived with an example already attached.

That has been the pattern so far. Ideas like completeness, occurrence and reliable evidence first made sense to me as practical questions. Is every slip here? Does this receipt actually support the claim? Why does the assessment differ from what was filed? The co-op term at AccTax Canada added bookkeeping to the list: how a bank reconciliation gets closed, and how a discrepancy is traced back to its source rather than adjusted away.

What keeps me interested is that the work has to hold up for someone else. A return, a reconciliation or a reimbursement package is only finished when another person can pick it up and rely on it. That is the standard I’m working toward.

02Direction

Where I’m headed

I’m aiming for public accounting, starting in tax. It is where my experience is deepest, and it is the area where I’ve seen most directly how careful work affects the decisions people make, both on this year’s return and in what they do next.

Over time I want a grounding in audit, financial reporting and advisory work as well, and I plan to pursue the CPA designation after I graduate.
