Work

Four short case studies.

Two from tax, one from bookkeeping, and one from a campus role. Each one is a problem I actually worked on, what I did about it, and what came out of it. No client details are included.

Case studies

Case 01 · Tax

CRA volunteer program, 2023–2025 · AccTax Canada, 2026

Is this return actually supported?

Personal tax preparation

The problem

A return is only as good as the documents behind it. Clients don’t always bring everything, and not every receipt supports what it seems to.

What I did

  1. Went through each client’s slips and receipts before entering anything, and asked about what was missing.
  2. Checked eligibility for deductions and credits, including RRSP receipts and medical expense claims.
  3. Prepared the return and explained the result to the client: which deductions, credits and benefits applied, and why.

At AccTax, the same work went to a senior for review, so I also investigated discrepancies and prepared the supporting documentation in ProFile.

Result

What I took from it

If the documents are complete, preparing the return is the quick part.

Case 02 · Tax

CRA volunteer program, 2023–2025

When the assessment doesn’t match the return

Notice of Assessment cross-checks

The problem

Sometimes the CRA’s Notice of Assessment comes back different from the return that was filed. The difference needs to be understood, and the file needs to be right before next year’s return.

What I did

  1. Compared the Notice of Assessment with the filed return.
  2. Found where the figures differed.
  3. Traced each difference back to the source documents.
  4. Updated the client’s file.

Result

Client files that match what the CRA actually assessed, and a better sense of how prior-year credits and deductions carry into the next return.

What I took from it

A difference isn’t always an error, but it always needs an explanation.

Case 03 · Bookkeeping

AccTax Canada, Hamilton · Jan.–Apr. 2026

Keeping small-business books reconciled

Bookkeeping in QuickBooks Desktop

The problem

Small-business clients need books that are current and agree with the bank. If transactions pile up, month-end gets harder and mistakes get harder to find.

What I did

  1. Recorded invoices and client transactions in QuickBooks Desktop day to day.
  2. Reconciled each client’s bank activity every month.
  3. Looked into differences instead of forcing the balance.

Result

Daily bookkeeping and monthly bank reconciliations for small-business clients, kept up alongside a full filing season of T1 work.

What I took from it

Bookkeeping is where the numbers everyone uses later actually come from.

Case 04 · Campus finance

Finance Coordinator, OT Media Pass · Sept. 2025 – present

Tracking a student organization’s money

Excel model and reimbursements

The problem

Events cost money and bring some in, and members pay for things out of pocket. The executive team needs to see where things stand, and reimbursements need proper support.

What I did

  1. Built an Excel model that tracks event costs and revenue drivers.
  2. Reconcile it every week.
  3. Put together monthly reimbursement packages with complete supporting documentation.
