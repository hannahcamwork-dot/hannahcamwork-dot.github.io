Exhibit 1

How a T1 file moves

  1. 01

    Client documents

    Slips, receipts and the prior-year Notice of Assessment are gathered and organized before anything is entered.

  2. 02

    Review

    The file is checked for missing information, eligibility issues and compliance risks.

  3. 03

    Validation

    Slips are matched to the client’s information; RRSP receipts, medical expenses and other claims are checked against their support.

  4. 04

    T1 preparation

    The return is prepared in ProFile or UFile, applying the deductions, credits and benefits the client is eligible for.

  5. 05

    Cross-check

    Figures are compared back to source documents. Anything unexplained is traced, and support is prepared for senior review.

  6. 06

    Filing†

    The return is finalized for filing with the CRA, and the result is explained to the client in plain terms.

†After assessment. When the Notice of Assessment arrives, it is compared against the filed return. Differences are traced to source documents and the file is updated.

A simplified view, based on my work with the CRA volunteer program and at AccTax Canada. In practice the steps overlap.

Case 01

Personal tax preparation

Context

Personal T1 returns for individual clients: first as a volunteer preparer handling returns from start to finish, then supporting a firm’s senior staff through the 2026 filing season.

Focus

Document collection and validation; deductions and credits; RRSP receipts and medical expense claims; employment-related deductions and rental income; Notices of Assessment; client questions.

What it involved

As a volunteer, I reviewed each client’s documents for missing information and eligibility issues, prepared the return, and explained the deductions, credits and benefits involved. After assessment, I compared Notices of Assessment with filed returns and traced any differences back to source documents.

At AccTax, the same work sat inside a review structure. I gathered and validated client information in ProFile, investigated discrepancies, and prepared supporting documentation for senior staff.

Outcome

Working knowledge of real Canadian personal tax filings and the documentation behind them, including what changes when a return will be reviewed by someone more senior. In the 2025 season, the returns I prepared contributed to more than $200,000 in client refunds.

Case 02

Small-business bookkeeping

Context

Daily bookkeeping for the firm’s small-business clients over a four-month co-op term.

Focus

Invoice processing, transaction processing and monthly bank reconciliations.

What it involved

Recording invoices and client transactions in QuickBooks Desktop as they came in, then reconciling each client’s bank activity at month-end, investigating differences rather than forcing the balance.

Outcome

A practical view of how accounting records are maintained before they become useful for reporting or tax, and how much of the later work depends on that upkeep being done carefully.
